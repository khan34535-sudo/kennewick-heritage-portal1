Kennewick Man · The Ancient One

8,500 years · Columbia River · Ancestor & Teacher

Welcome, Relatives

This space honors The Ancient One — known to science as Kennewick Man. He is not an artifact or a specimen. He is an ancestor, a teacher, and a reminder that Indigenous peoples have lived on this land since time immemorial. He was repatriated and reburied in 2017 by the five claimant tribes.

A Living Land Acknowledgement

This page is dedicated to The Ancient One, an ancestor of the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation, the Nez Perce Tribe, the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation, the Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakama Nation, and the Wanapum People.

He was returned to the earth in 2017 after twenty years of legal struggle. His reburial was an act of spiritual law, not an archaeological event. Out of respect, this page does not display his remains or his likeness.

  • The Columbia River is a relative — a provider, a spiritual being, and the ancestral lifeline of the Plateau peoples.
  • Camas, bitterroot, and salmon are sacred foods that sustained generations since time immemorial.
  • Cedar carries prayers. Its smoke purifies, protects, and carries messages to the Creator.
  • His story is not ours to tell. It belongs to the tribes. This page only honors it.

Discovery · Who, What, Where, When, How

Who: Two college students, Will Thomas and Dave Deacy, noticed a human skull in the mud while sneaking into hydroplane races along the Columbia River.

What: A nearly complete skeleton — one of the oldest and most complete ever found in North America, with over 380 bones and fragments.

Where: The shallows of the Columbia River, near Kennewick, Washington, on land managed by the US Army Corps of Engineers (Columbia Park).

When: Discovered on July 28, 1996. Radiocarbon dating placed the skeleton at approximately 8,500 years old.

How: A local forensic anthropologist, James Chatters, recovered the skeleton and discovered a stone spearpoint embedded in the hip. The bone had healed around it — he survived the wound.

Radiocarbon Dating

Radiocarbon dating of a bone sample was performed at the University of California, Riverside. Results placed the skeleton at 8,340–9,200 calibrated years before present.

      Significance · History, Law, Legacy

      Legal Timeline

      • 1996: Discovery. Tribes claim him under NAGPRA.
      • 1996–2004: Eight scientists sue the government. Courts rule NAGPRA does not apply.
      • 2005–2006: Scientists study the bones at the Burke Museum.
      • 2015: Genome sequencing proves Native American ancestry.
      • 2016: US government determines the Ancient One is Native American; repatriation begins.
      • 2017: Reburial by the five claimant tribes.

      Key Figures

      • Will Thomas & Dave Deacy — Discoverers (1996)
      • James Chatters — Forensic anthropologist
      • Dr. Douglas Owsley — Smithsonian bioarchaeologist
      • Dr. Eske Willerslev — Geneticist (2015)
      • Five Claimant Tribes — Colville, Nez Perce, Umatilla, Yakama, Wanapum
